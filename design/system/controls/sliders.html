Sliders, readouts and the one checkbox

Speed and Volume are the same control twice: a word in --dim, a native range in accent-color amber, and a readout in body ink. Everything a slider does is native — no track, no thumb, no custom drawing — and everything the app adds is the box around the number. The bpm readout is also an input: a contenteditable <b>, not a text field, so it stays a reading until you click it.

The tempo track is 110 px and the volume track 64 — two fixed widths, not a clamp and not a share of the row. A slider never shrinks: in the transport bar the status line is the only thing that gives, so a level keeps the same reach on every page and at every window width, and a drag means the same tempo change wherever you do it. accent-color is the only paint the app puts on the control, so the track, the thumb and the fill are the browser's and the colour is ours.

Each dashed rule is the right edge of the widest row in its own stack, and the narrow value lands on it too — the row does not move when the number grows a digit. Tabular figures stop the digits from dancing; the reserve is what stops the row from moving. With box-sizing:border-box a min-width:35px leaves 25 px of content, so 60 and 200 both measure 35 px and the word bpm never shifts. Volume is the same story one size up: 7% and 100% both sit on min-width:47px, where 100% on its own measures 46.60. The reserve is the widest value the readout can reach, not the width it usually has — the earlier 30 px and 38 px were each one digit short, and the bar moved the moment the tempo passed 99 or the volume hit 100%.

Melody — the one checkbox in the app
rest13 px body ink, gap:5px, a native 13 px box in accent-color amber. 18.84 px tall against 30 px buttons
checkedthe browser's own tick on the amber ground. The app paints nothing — there is no custom box anywhere
.offthis track has no melody: text to --dim and the cursor back to default on both label and box. Not disabled, not faded
Melody is the one control in the whole app whose label sits to the right of it. Everywhere else the name is on the left of the thing it names (Speed, Volume) or inside it on its own plate (Out:). It reads that way because it is a checkbox and the whole label is the hit target — but it means the bar has three label idioms in one row.
Phone Learn — a stepper instead of a slider
60bpm
A 40 px row, 8 px radius. Tempo becomes two 40 × 40 targets around a 700 14px mono value with a 10 px bpm beside it — a slider is not a thing you can set to 72 with a thumb. The stepper's own buttons have no border and no radius: the strip's overflow:hidden cuts the corners, exactly as the Out strip does. Learn has no volume slider: the control was scraped.

Rules. A level is a native range with accent-color:var(--accent) and nothing else painted on it; the app never draws a track or a thumb. Its name goes to the left in 12 px --dim, its value to the right in body ink, 7 px apart, on one line that never wraps. A value is always tabular and always right-aligned, and it lives in a 4 px box with a transparent 1 px border so the box can be painted on hover without moving anything. A readout you can type into says so only on hover (border --line) and only commits to amber while it has focus, where it also goes centred; a readout you cannot type into keeps cursor:default and never paints a border. The reserve on a readout has to be the widest value it can reach, not the width it usually has — 35 px for three bpm digits and 47 px for 100%, because the earlier 30 and 38 were each a digit short and the row shifted when the number grew. Where a slider will not do, the phone swaps the control rather than shrinking it: two 40 px targets and a mono value, one strip, corners cut once.
