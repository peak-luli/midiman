Transport bar

The one row across the top of all three laptop pages, in the markup the pages actually carry. It is a flex row of groups: a group is a fixed set of whole controls that belong together, 6 px apart, and groups are 14 px apart. It is one row at laptop width and wraps only below it. Seven slots, left to right — transport, output, page, readout, levels, input, status — and the three pages differ only in what goes in page and whether Stop is in the transport.

Practice — index.html, 1240 px
Out: – Speed 100 bpm Volume 100% – connecting…
The bar's own markup, with only #outsel's inner HTML written out by hand — in the page that span is empty and src/outtoggle.js fills it. Two controls carry hidden here as they do at rest: the ♪ melody-sound toggle in page, which appears once the track has a melody, and (on learn) the • tempo mark. A group of one control needs no .grp span at all — #outsel, #pos and #statusEl are bare children of the bar and take the 14 px group gap directly.
Looper — looper.html, 1240 px
Out: – Speed 100 bpm Volume 100% – connecting…
Same seven slots. page holds one control here — ♩ Backing, lit, because the backing track starts on — so the row is the practice row with one control fewer and everything right of page sitting further left, which the status line takes back.
Learn — learn.html, 1240 px
Out: – Speed 60 bpm Volume 100% – connecting…
Learn has no Stop — the transport is Play and Click — and its page group is two toggles, ⏸ Wait for me and ↻ Loop. The group is wider than the practice one and the whole right-hand side moves with it; the slots stay in the same order, which is the only thing that has to hold across the three pages.
The gap rule
▶ Play6 ■ Stop6 ● Click14 Out: Piano Computer14 Melody
Two distances and no others. 6 px means "these are one control between them": you read Play·Stop·Click as one transport, and the eye does not stop inside it. 14 px means "a different job starts here". There is no third gap and no separator hairline — the .sep spans that used to sit between the transport and the readouts are gone, because a gap more than twice the other one already says what they said.
The status line is the one thing that gives
Out: – Speed 100 bpm Volume 100% – out: computer audio · MIDI blocked: Permission to use Web MIDI API was not granted by the user.
Exactly one slot gives way, and it is always the same one. #statusEl is flex:1 1 0;min-width:0, right-aligned, with text-overflow:ellipsis; every other slot is flex:0 0 auto and keeps the width its contents ask for. A MIDI error long enough to break the row loses its own tail instead, and no control moves by a pixel. When even a truncated status line is not enough — below laptop width — #bar's flex-wrap:wrap takes a second row rather than clipping a readout. The slack is thin and it is all held here on purpose. Measured on a 1240 px bar, the status line gets 66.41 px on practice, 36.70 on looper and 19.28 on learn — which is why even connecting… arrives with an ellipsis. Every other slot is full size in all three; the whole of the difference between the pages lands in this one span, and it is the right place for it, because a truncated status line still reads and a squeezed readout would not.
The readouts hold their width
Out: – Speed 100 bpm Volume 100% – out: computer audio
Out: bar 12/12 · Bb7 Speed 100 bpm Volume 100% C#4 E4 G#4 out: computer audio
The two readouts are the only things in the bar whose text changes while you play, and both are given a box rather than left to their content: #pos is tabular with min-width:102px, and #played is mono, tabular and width:100px with the overflow clipped (Learn, which has the room, widens it to 128). Nothing but those two readouts differs between the two rows, and the dashed line is the left edge of the levels group — the first thing that would move. #played holds: a held C#4 E4 G#4 measures 90.00 px, and past three notes heldLabel counts the rest instead of printing them — C#4 D#4 F#4 +7, 118.02 px, inside Learn's 128 px box and clipped in the shared 100. A reserve has to be the widest string the readout can print, not the one it usually prints, which is why #pos reserves 102 px; the bpm and volume readouts follow the same rule and reserve 35 and 47 px (see Controls · Sliders). And a reserve is only half of it: a readout that can also exceed its reserve moves the row anyway, which is what ten fingers on the piano used to do here — so the box is a width, not a floor, and what will not fit is trimmed before it arrives and clipped if it still does not. Learn holds #pos the same way, at width:192px, because its longest string — bar 128 · beat 4 · pass 100, 189.25 px — is nearly twice the 102 the shared floor reserves.

Rules. The bar is one row at laptop width: 8 px above and below, 14 px at each end, a 1 px --line rule under it, and every control centred on the one line. It is built of groups, not of controls: 6 px inside a group, 14 px between groups, no hairlines and no third distance. The seven slots are fixed and in this order — transport, output, page, readout, levels, input, status — and a page may change what is in a slot but never the order, and never adds a slot. A group of one control needs no wrapper. Every control in the bar is 30 px: buttons, the icon toggle and the Out strip alike; only the plain readings (the checkbox, the numbers, the LED) are shorter, and they are centred, not aligned. One thing gives way: the status line, truncating with an ellipsis; and when that is not enough, below laptop width, the row wraps. Nothing is ever clipped and nothing else moves — every other slot is flex:0 0 auto. Anything whose text changes while the app runs holds a reserve and is set in tabular figures, so that a bar number or a chord never shoves the row: what it eats instead is the slack the status line is holding.
